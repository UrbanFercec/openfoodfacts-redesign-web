Green-Score

The Green-Score logos

1What is the Green Score?

The Green-Score is a logo that aims to inform you about the environmental impact of food.

The colour code varies from dark green (A+) for the least impactful products to dark red (F) for the most impactful products.

2Which product is better for the planet?

It can be hard to tell

Choosing sustainable food is one of the most powerful things we can do for the climate, as our global food system is responsible for over a quarter of all greenhouse gas emissions. (Source: Poore & Nemecek, Science, 2018).

You have the power!

The good news is that this gives us a powerful way to create change. Each of us eats around 1,000 meals every year. That’s 1,000 opportunities to choose a better future for the planet!

Transparency

Unlike proprietary labels, the Green-Score calculation is completely open and can be verified by anyone.

3The core: Life cycle assessment (LCA)

A scientific evaluation of food categories

For each product category, a benchmark score is established using data from the Agribalyse environmental database (designed by ADEME and INRAE).

Life cycle analysis is a standardized assessment method for carrying out a multi-stage and multi-criteria environmental assessment.

4Bonuses & penalties

To reward better products within a category, we then apply bonuses & penalties based on several criterion:

Mode of production

A bonus is awarded to products that have an official label, a label or a certification that guarantees environmental benefits (organic, fair trade, HVE, Label Rouge, Bleu Blanc Cœur, MSC/ASC).

Origin of ingredients

A bonus is awarded based on the origin of the ingredients. This bonus takes into account the impact on transportation and also the environmental policy of each producer’s country.

Endangered species

A penalty is given to products that contain ingredients that have significant negative impacts on biodiversity and ecosystems, such as palm oil, the production of which is responsible for massive deforestation.

Packaging

A penalty is calculated to take into account the circularity of packaging (use of recycled raw material and recyclability) and overpacking.

5An increased need for transparency to better measure and reduce environmental impacts
